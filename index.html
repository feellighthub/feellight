How Somatic Coaching Works

Somatic coaching starts with a specific challenge you want to work through. Together, we explore what is happening emotionally, mentally, and in the body, identify where you are in the process, and determine what kind of support is most appropriate for you right now.

For example, after a painful breakup, your needs may look very different depending on where you are in the process. In the beginning, the focus may be on grounding, stabilizing your nervous system, and rebuilding your internal resources. Later, we may work with anger, grief, emotional attachment, or difficulty letting go. As you move forward, the work can shift toward releasing what remains unresolved, reconnecting with yourself, and creating emotional space for new relationships and experiences.

Another example is anger. Some people suppress anger until it turns inward, while others react before they have time to choose how they want to respond. Somatic work helps you build a greater capacity to experience strong emotions without suppressing them or immediately acting on them. Through personalized body-based practices, you learn to notice, contain, process, and express emotions in healthier and more constructive ways.

The practice is tailored to you and may evolve as your needs change. The goal is not simply to understand a pattern intellectually, but to work with how that pattern is experienced and expressed through both mind and body.

How Constellations Work

A constellation brings your situation out of your head and into a visual space. Using carefully selected symbolic images and figures, we create a physical representation of the people, relationships, choices, or other elements involved in your situation.

Seeing the situation in front of you creates psychological distance and allows you to step into the role of an observer. This can make relationships, tensions, patterns, and connections easier to recognize than when you are experiencing the situation entirely from within.

Throughout the process, I guide you with focused questions and observations rather than interpreting the situation for you. I also pay attention to emotional and physical responses that arise as you interact with different elements of the constellation. The abstract and symbolic nature of the process can open new ways of looking at a situation and bring forward thoughts, feelings, and associations that may not emerge through conversation alone.

Personal & Relationship Constellations

Personal work can focus on a decision, an internal conflict, a recurring pattern, a difficult life transition, or an area where you feel stuck. Relationship work explores your experience in connection with a partner, family member, friend, or another significant person. For example, we might explore why the same relationship pattern keeps repeating, what makes a particular decision feel impossible, or what may be underneath a persistent emotional reaction.

The purpose is not to tell you what decision to make or what another person is thinking. It is to help you see your own experience and the dynamics surrounding it more clearly, including aspects that may have been difficult to recognize from inside the situation.

ㅤ

Family Constellations

In family work, we create a visual representation of relevant family members and relationships within the family system. We can explore patterns of closeness and distance, unresolved conflicts, roles, boundaries, loyalties, and recurring dynamics across relationships. Seeing the system as a whole can provide a very different perspective from looking at each relationship separately.

ㅤ

Business Constellations

Business constellations expand the same approach to an organizational system. The elements may include people, teams, clients, products, processes, resources, locations, goals, or other parts of the business. This allows us to map a complex situation visually and explore how its different elements relate to one another.

Business constellation work can be particularly useful when you are facing a difficult decision, experiencing a persistent obstacle, navigating change, or trying to understand why something within the organization is not working as expected. It can also be used to explore different directions before committing to a particular strategy.

ㅤ

After the Session

A constellation often gives you material to continue reflecting on after the session. New connections, emotions, or perspectives may become clearer as you process the experience. Rather than forcing an immediate conclusion, the work can become a starting point for further reflection, decisions, conversations, or personal change.

How Shadow Work Works

Carl Jung described the shadow as the parts of ourselves we may reject, suppress, or simply not recognize, including emotions, desires, reactions, qualities, and strengths. Shadow work brings these parts into awareness and helps integrate them into a more complete sense of self.

Our work follows a carefully crafted questionnaire that I developed through more than 15 years of personal exploration, study, and work with others. Together, we explore relationships, emotional patterns, triggers, boundaries, self-perception, work, money, and other areas of life.

Some questions can go deep and bring up emotions, experiences, or truths that may be difficult to face. I provide support, reflection, and focused questions to help you understand and process what comes up. Somatic practices may also be incorporated when helpful.

ㅤ

One-on-One Shadow Work

Individual sessions give you dedicated attention, privacy, and a flexible pace. We can spend more time where you need it, go deeper when something important comes up, and schedule sessions around your availability.

ㅤ

Small Group Shadow Work

Groups are limited to four participants and meet weekly at a consistent time. Everyone receives individual attention and equal space to participate while choosing how much they want to share.

Working in a group also lets you learn from other people’s experiences. You begin to see that many things you may feel ashamed of, confused about, or alone in are actually part of a shared human experience.

Confidentiality is required. What is shared in the group stays in the group, creating a safe space for openness, honesty, and practicing healthy boundaries.

ㅤ

What You Can Gain From Shadow Work

The work can help you build stronger self-worth, healthier boundaries, greater emotional awareness, and more authentic relationships. You become clearer about who you are, what you need, what you value, and how you want to respond to the people and situations in your life.

Once you begin to recognize your own shadow, you also start seeing shadow patterns in other people. You understand your own triggers better, recognize what may be behind other people’s reactions, and become less likely to react automatically.

The process can be challenging, but deeply rewarding. The goal is to feel more whole, more confident in yourself, and more grounded in the way you relate to yourself and others.

About Regina

My Story

My path into this work started in my early twenties. What began as a personal need to better understand myself gradually became a lifelong interest in psychology, human behavior, relationships, spirituality, and personal growth.
For more than 15 years, I have continued exploring these areas through personal work, study, training, and working with others. Even while building a career in a completely different field, this was the work I kept coming back to.
For many years, most of my personal work was focused on the mind. I learned to understand my patterns, where they came from, and why I reacted in certain ways. That work gave me a tremendous amount of insight. But eventually I reached a point where I understood a lot and still felt stuck. I could explain a pattern perfectly and still repeat it.
That was when my focus began to shift toward the body. I started exploring somatic practices, the nervous system, emotions, physical responses, and the connection between what we experience mentally and what happens in the body. My own experience with persistent physical symptoms also became an important part of this exploration.

ㅤ

Why I Created FeelLight Studio

Through this process, I became especially interested in the difference between top-down and bottom-up approaches.
Top-down work starts with the mind. We use awareness, reflection, conversation, and understanding to explore our thoughts, beliefs, experiences, and patterns. Bottom-up work starts with the body and works with physical sensations, breath, movement, sound, emotions, and the nervous system.
Both are valuable. But in my own search, I found it surprisingly difficult to find approaches that truly combined the two. Some practices gave me a great intellectual understanding of what was happening but did very little with the body. Others offered powerful body-based experiences but did not always help me understand how those experiences connected to my history, relationships, or patterns.
There is now much more awareness of the mind-body connection. Books such as The Body Keeps the Score have helped bring these ideas into mainstream conversation. But reading and understanding the connection is different from actually working with it. That difference became very important to me.
Over time, through a lot of study, experimentation, and personal experience, I began bringing the two sides together. I found that sometimes we need to understand what is happening, and sometimes understanding is not enough. We may also need to work with how an experience shows up in the body and nervous system.
That became the foundation of FeelLight Studio: integrating top-down and bottom-up approaches for real change.
Depending on the person and the challenge, our work may include reflection and self-exploration together with somatic practices, breathwork, meditation, movement, voice and sound, symbolic work, or systemic constellations. We start with what is happening in your life and choose the tools that make sense for you rather than trying to fit everyone into the same method.

ㅤ

Background & Training

My academic and professional background is actually very different from this work. I have an engineering degree, an MBA, and graduate-level education in marketing. My engineering background has strongly influenced the way I think. I naturally look for patterns, connections, systems, and practical ways to understand complex problems.
At the same time, my personal studies have always been centered on people: psychology, relationships, spirituality, personal development, and the connection between mind and body. Over the years, I have explored both Western and Eastern perspectives and many different approaches to personal growth.
I am a certified Kundalini Yoga teacher and have taught for several years. I see Kundalini Yoga as a practical mind-body tool that can use breathwork, movement, meditation, mantra, and kriya to work with emotions, attention, and the body. I incorporate elements of these practices when they are appropriate for the person and the work we are doing.
I am also trained in private one-on-one constellation work using symbolic images and figures. This allows us to explore personal, relationship, family, and business systems without requiring a traditional group constellation. My continuing studies also include somatic and psychosomatic approaches and the relationship between emotional experience, the nervous system, and the body.
FeelLight Studio brings together what I have learned from more than 15 years of personal exploration, education, training, and practice. At some point, I realized I had accumulated more knowledge and experience than I wanted to keep only for myself. People around me were already coming to me for perspective and support, and creating FeelLight Studio felt like a natural next step: a place where I could share what I have learned and help others find their own way forward.
